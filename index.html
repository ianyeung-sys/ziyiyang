About

01

I work at the intersection of AI, intelligent systems, and product development — and I care most about the point where an idea becomes something someone can actually use.

I am an Electronic Information Engineering student at HKUST. Most of what I build starts the same way: a problem I have run into myself, or one I have watched someone else struggle with.

That has led me in a few different directions — AI-powered educational tools, intelligent hardware, web products, and research work on signal processing. I like the range. Building an embedded system and building a web product ask different questions, and moving between them keeps me honest about what actually matters in each.

My approach is simple: start with a real problem, build something tangible, test it with real users, and keep improving it.

Experience

02

  1. What I worked on

    • Evaluated and classified AI-generated scientific and visual content.
    • Refined evaluation criteria for AI-generated research content.
    • Summarised evaluation results for use across the team.
    • Achieved the highest monthly output within the team.

    What I learned

    • How much the quality of an AI system depends on the clarity of the criteria used to judge its output.
    • That writing an evaluation standard someone else can apply consistently is a design problem, not a paperwork task.
    • How to keep throughput high without letting consistency drift.

  2. What I worked on

    • Worked on an AI-oriented educational application.
    • Participated in application testing and user testing.
    • Gathered feedback and improved application interactions.
    • Contributed to the development of an educational tool for children with special needs.

    What I learned

    • When users have different needs from the ones you assumed, testing tells you — guessing does not. Most of the improvements came directly from watching people use it.
